Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to the ability of machines and computer systems to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. These tasks include learning from experience, understanding language, recognizing patterns, solving problems, and making decisions. AI is not a single technology but a broad field that combines computer science, mathematics, data science, and cognitive science.

At its core, AI works by using algorithms and data. Modern AI systems, especially those based on machine learning (ML), learn patterns from large amounts of data instead of being explicitly programmed for every task. A subset of machine learning called deep learning uses neural networks inspired by the human brain to process complex data such as images, audio, and text. This approach has enabled major breakthroughs in areas like speech recognition, image classification, and natural language processing.

AI is already part of everyday life. Virtual assistants like Siri and Google Assistant, recommendation systems on YouTube and Netflix, navigation apps, spam filters, and facial recognition systems all rely on AI. In healthcare, AI helps in disease detection, medical imaging analysis, and drug discovery. In finance, it is used for fraud detection, algorithmic trading, and credit scoring. Industries such as manufacturing, education, agriculture, and transportation are also rapidly adopting AI to improve efficiency and accuracy.

There are different types of AI. Narrow AI (or Weak AI) is designed to perform a specific task, such as chatbots or image recognition systems. Most AI today falls into this category. General AI (or Strong AI), which can think and learn like a human across many tasks, is still theoretical and not yet achieved.
